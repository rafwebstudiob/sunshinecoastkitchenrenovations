Kitchen layouts for renovations on the Sunshine Coast

In short: The right layout depends on the room, the rest of the house and how you use the kitchen. Keeping plumbing where it is usually saves the most.

A plain-English guide to the common layouts, then a free form to reach one local kitchen renovator.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

Galley and U-shaped kitchens

A galley kitchen runs along one or two parallel walls. It's efficient for smaller spaces and common in units and older homes. A U-shaped kitchen wraps around three walls and gives plenty of bench and storage space if the room is wide enough to move around comfortably.

L-shaped and open-plan kitchens

An L-shaped kitchen uses two walls and opens onto a dining or living area. It's a popular choice when a renovation opens up a closed-off kitchen, which may involve removing a wall. If that wall is load-bearing, you'll need a builder and, usually, approval.

Island kitchens and butler's pantries

An island adds bench space, storage and often seating. It needs enough clearance around it to work well. A butler's pantry, or scullery, behind the main kitchen keeps appliances and mess out of sight and has become common in new homes.

What moves the budget

Keeping the sink, cooktop and dishwasher where they are is usually the biggest saving. Moving plumbing, gas or electrics, or changing walls, adds trades and time. See our kitchen renovation cost guide.
