The kitchen renovation process on the Sunshine Coast

In short: A kitchen renovation runs from brief and design to approvals, demolition, rough-in, installation and handover. In Queensland, the QBCC says work over $3,300 needs a written contract and home warranty cover, with deposits capped at 10% (under $20,000) or 5% ($20,000 or more).

A step-by-step guide with the official rules for your state. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

This guide explains a typical kitchen renovation and the official rules that apply on the Sunshine Coast. It is not legal advice. Follow the links, and ask the renovator to confirm what's needed for your home.

1. Brief and budget

Decide what's wrong with the current kitchen, what you want to keep, and your budget, including a contingency. Collect photos of kitchens you like.

2. Measure, design and quote

The renovator measures up, talks through layout and materials, and prepares a design and a written quote. Check what's included: demolition, plumbing, electrical, splashback, flooring, painting, appliances and rubbish removal.

3. Approvals and contract

In Queensland, check that whoever contracts for the job holds a QBCC licence that covers the work, and that plumbing and electrical work is done by licensed trades. Removing or changing structural walls usually needs building approval from a certifier.

Source: QBCC, Domestic building contracts.

In Queensland, the QBCC says domestic building work over $3,300 needs a written contract, and the contractor must arrange Queensland Home Warranty Scheme cover. The deposit before work starts can be no more than 10% for contracts from $3,301 to $19,999, or 5% for contracts of $20,000 or more. Contracts over $3,300 also come with a cooling-off period (generally five business days). Check the contractor's QBCC licence before you sign.

Sources: QBCC, Domestic building contracts; QBCC, Deposits and progress payments; QBCC, Cooling-off period.

4. Demolition and rough-in

The old kitchen comes out, then the plumber and electrician run new services before walls are patched. Homes built before about 1990 may contain asbestos, which must be handled safely by people qualified to do it.

5. Installation and finishing

Cabinets, benchtops, splashback, appliances, tapware and lighting go in, followed by final connections and finishing. Expect to be without a working kitchen for part of the job, so ask for a timeline.

6. Handover

Walk through the finished kitchen with the renovator, note any defects in writing, and keep the compliance certificates for plumbing and electrical work and any warranty documents.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources before relying on this summary.
